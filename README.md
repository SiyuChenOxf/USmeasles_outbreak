# Spatial Clustering of School Susceptibles Drives Divergent US Measles Outbreaks
County-level vaccination data mask the sub-county spatial clustering of susceptible individuals that drives measles outbreak dynamics. We quantify this masking in the two largest US measles outbreaks in over two decades. Despite similar sub-95% K–12 MMR coverage, peak susceptible density in Spartanburg County, South Carolina was 5.7 times that of Gaines County, Texas (23 vs. 4 unvaccinated students per square mile; 95% CI 2.4-12.5) – a difference that separated self-limiting spread from persistent transmission chains. Where susceptibles cluster, not how many exist, determined outbreak fate. Our framework identifies high-risk micro-geographies invisible to aggregate surveillance and argues for routine school-level spatial monitoring to guide targeted intervention.## Data
